*   **Type:** peer-reviewed article
*   **Relevance:** P20 (authors now confirmed). CO2-regression forced component plus autoregressive internal component as a decadal temperature benchmark.

## Contribution

*   **Research problem:** Provide a simple empirical benchmark for decadal near-surface temperature predictions from AOGCMs.
*   **Method:** Linear regression of temperature onto CO2 forcing (forcing linearly extrapolated beyond the initial time) plus an autoregressive model for internal variability; separation of components uses IPCC AR4 twentieth-century integrations.
*   **Data or materials:** Near-surface temperature; IPCC AR4 20th-century model integrations for component separation.
*   **Main result:** Cross-validated correlation 0.84 and RMS error 0.12 K for global-mean temperature 10 years ahead.

## Quotes

1.  "It is assumed that the temperature time series can be decomposed into components related to external forcing and internal variability. The considered external forcing consists of the atmospheric CO2 concentration." (abstract)
    *   Supports: the model splits temperature into a CO2-forced part and internal variability.
2.  "Separation of the two components is achieved by using the Intergovernmental Panel on Climate Change Fourth Assessment Report (IPCC AR4) twentieth-century integrations." (abstract)
    *   Supports: the method uses climate-model (AR4 20th-century) runs to separate components.
3.  "Temperature anomalies due to changing external forcing are described by a linear regression onto the forcing. The future evolution of the external forcing that is needed for predictions is approximated by a linear extrapolation of the forcing prior to the initial time. Temperature anomalies owing to the internal variability are described by an autoregressive model." (abstract)
    *   Supports: forcing is extrapolated linearly from pre-initial data and internal variability is AR.
4.  "An evaluation of hindcast experiments shows that the empirical model has a cross-validated correlation skill of 0.84 and a cross-validated rms error of 0.12 K in hindcasting globalmean temperature anomalies 10 years ahead." (abstract; "globalmean" as extracted)
    *   Supports: reported skill for global-mean temperature at 10-yr lead.

## Not Covered

*   City or station scale, precipitation, extremes, degree days, or 1-7 yr leads specifically.
*   Use of AR4 model integrations may conflict with a rule excluding climate-model output; the integrations are twentieth-century runs, and the paper's exact dependence was not examined in detail.
