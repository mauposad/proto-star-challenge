# mckinnon2024pace

*   **Citation:** McKinnon, K. A., Simpson, I. R., & Williams, A. P. (2024). The pace of change of summertime temperature extremes. Proceedings of the National Academy of Sciences 121 (published October 7, 2024).
*   **Identifier:** DOI 10.1073/pnas.2406143121; NSF Public Access Repository — https://par.nsf.gov/servlets/purl/10571122
*   **Retrieved:** 2026-11-09
*   **Access:** full text (`sources/mckinnon2024pace.pdf`, `sources/mckinnon2024pace.txt`; an identical earlier download is at `sources/par10571122.pdf`)
*   **Type:** peer-reviewed article
*   **Relevance:** V05 in papers.csv. The linked file is this paper on summer temperature extremes, not a study of hourly versus daily cooling demand as papers.csv says. Relevant to hottest-day trends. Its analysis uses observations through 2023 and CMIP6, so treat it as background on trends, not as a source of forecast inputs.

## Contribution

*   **Research problem:** Whether hot summer extremes warm faster than typical summer days.
*   **Method:** Compare warming of hot/cold summer-day quantiles vs. median in observations and CMIP6 historical runs; surface energy balance explanation.
*   **Data or materials:** Observations 1959-2023; 262 simulations across 28 CMIP6 models.
*   **Main result:** Hottest summer days warmed at the same pace as the median globally, by hemisphere and in the tropics; the coldest summer days warmed more slowly.

## Quotes

1.  "Here, we show that, in both observations and historical climate model simulations, the hottest summer days have warmed at the same pace as the median globally, in each hemisphere, and in the tropics from 1959 to 2023." (abstract)
    *   Supports: no hot-day amplification relative to the median at large scales, 1959-2023.
2.  "In contrast, the coldest summer days have warmed more slowly than the median in the global average, a signal that is not simulated in any of 262 simulations across 28 CMIP6 models." (abstract)
    *   Supports: cold-tail stretching in observations not reproduced by models.
3.  "Tropical hot day amplification is projected to emerge in the future (2024–2099, SSP3-7.0 scenario), while Northern Hemisphere heat extremes are expected to continue to follow the median." (abstract)
    *   Supports: projected behavior; NH extremes expected to follow the median.
