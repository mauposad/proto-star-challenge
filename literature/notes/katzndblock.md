## Contribution

*   **Research problem:** Extreme value analysis of block maxima under nonstationarity.
*   **Method:** GEV with covariate-dependent parameters fit by maximum likelihood.
*   **Data or materials:** Example: summer minimum temperature at Phoenix, AZ.
*   **Main result:** not stated (tutorial).

## Quotes

1.  "No general extreme value theory under nonstationarity" (slide 34)
    *   Supports: theory caveat for nonstationary extremes.
2.  "Introduction of covariates resembles “generalized linear models”" (slide 34)
    *   Supports: covariates enter GEV parameters similarly to GLMs.
3.  "Straightforward to extend maximum likelihood estimation" (slide 34)
    *   Supports: ML estimation extends to nonstationary GEV.
4.  "μ(t) = μ0 + μ1 t, ln σ(t) = σ0 + σ1 t, ξ(t) = ξ, t = 1, 2, . . ." (slide 35, Phoenix example)
    *   Supports: linear trend in location and log-linear trend in scale with constant shape.

## Not Covered

*   Annual maximum Tmax specifically (the worked example uses negated summer minima); forecasting skill.
