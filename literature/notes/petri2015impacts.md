# petri2015impacts

*   **Citation:** Petri, Y., & Caldeira, K. (2015). Impacts of global warming on residential heating and cooling degree-days in the United States. Scientific Reports 5, 12427.
*   **Identifier:** DOI 10.1038/srep12427; PMCID PMC4523835 — https://doi.org/10.1038/srep12427
*   **Retrieved:** 2026-11-09
*   **Access:** full text (Europe PMC JATS XML, `sources/petri2015impacts.xml`; tag-stripped text `sources/petri2015impacts.txt`, in which reference numbers run into adjacent words; an identical earlier download is at `sources/pmc4523835.xml`)
*   **Type:** peer-reviewed article
*   **Relevance:** CDD climatology across U.S. stations and cities, with a cited sensitivity range for cooling per 1 °C. Its projections use CMIP5 output, so they are context only and not usable as forecast inputs.

## Contribution

*   **Research problem:** Projected changes in residential HDD and CDD in the U.S.
*   **Method:** Add CMIP5 RCP8.5 median projected changes to NOAA 1981-2010 station degree-day normals.
*   **Data or materials:** NOAA 1981-2010 normals for 7,438 stations; CMIP5.
*   **Main result:** CDD rises everywhere by 2080-2099; HDD+CDD falls in the North and rises in the South.

## Quotes

1.  "concluding that the total projected average across the surveyed national studies is a 9.2% energy reduction for heating and a 5–20% increase for cooling per 1 °C of temperature increase." (introduction, summarizing a US Climate Change Science Program synthesis)
    *   Supports: cited sensitivity of cooling energy to 1 °C warming (energy, not CDD).
2.  "In the last 20 years, US heating degree-days (HDD) declined and cooling degree-days (CDD) increased due to warming trends" (introduction)
    *   Supports: observed recent direction of U.S. HDD/CDD changes.
3.  "These factors, however, are not taken into account if the standard US base temperature" (introduction; continues "of 65 °F (18.3 °C) is used.", with reference numbers between)
    *   Supports: standard CDD uses a fixed 65 °F base.
4.  "Among these cities, in the historical period (1981–2010), San Francisco, CA, has the lowest CDD value (163 CDD), while Phoenix, AZ, has the highest CDD value (4,608 CDD)." (Results, 25 US cities)
    *   Supports: CDD spans more than an order of magnitude across U.S. cities.

## Not Covered

*   CDD sensitivity per degree derived from observations; forecasting methods.
