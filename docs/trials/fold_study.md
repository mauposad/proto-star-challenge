# Fold design study: 3 ADR 0004 origins against 12 rolling origins (P10, P12, P19)

**Question.** Do the three equal-weight ADR 0004 cut-offs (1980, 1995, 2011) rank methods the
same way as a denser set of rolling origins before 2019? And would giving recent origins more
weight change which method we pick? The second question came from
[leaderboard_live_2026-11-07.md](leaderboard_live_2026-11-07.md), which was used as context
only. **No leaderboard number went into any computation, weight or choice here.**

**Short answer.** In years 4–7 (the Final Score window) and over all years, the two designs
rank methods almost the same way (Kendall tau-b 0.93). `hybrid_ridge_all_cdd` comes first under
every design and weighting we tried, in more than 99 % of origin-bootstrap resamples in years 4–7.
Recency weighting does not change the pick. It changes only the runner-up over all years and
the top pick in years 1–3, where every design is unstable. The three ADR origins overstate the
*level* of every score: `current_best` averages 4.68 in years 4–7 on the ADR origins and 3.21 on
the 12. The proposed decision is in [ADR 0006](../decisions/0006-fold-design-dense-origins.md)
(status: proposed).

All tables come from [fold_study_results.md](fold_study_results.md). It is generated by
`scripts/fold_study_p10_p12_p19.py report` and also has per-origin ranks, leave-one-out tables
and the warming diagnostics for every window.

With 8 methods, tau 0.93 means one swapped pair out of 28. In years 4–7 that pair is
`current_best` / `gev_stationary`. They are 0.01 apart on the ADR origins and differ only in the
hottest-day column. Years 1–3 disagree much more: `arima_temp_cdd` is 2nd on the ADR origins
and 5th on the dense set, and `current_best` moves from 5th to 2nd.

The origins overlap: with 3-year steps and a 7-year horizon, each test year is scored from 2 or
3 origins. The resamples are therefore not independent, and these shares overstate stability
somewhat. The years 4–7 result holds by a wide margin all the same.

## What changes under recency weighting (weights fixed before the run)

- **Years 4–7:** nothing at the top. `hybrid_ridge_all_cdd` stays first, ahead of `hybrid_a`
  by 0.51 (linear) and 0.53 (exponential). Its gain over `current_best` is +0.77 equal-weight
  and +0.73 exponential. Lower down, `ten_year_mean` passes `arima_temp_cdd`.
- **All years:** the pick is unchanged. `current_best` passes `hybrid_a` for second place
  (3.79 against 3.74 exponential).
- **Years 1–3:** the pick moves from `hybrid_a` to `current_best` (2.57 against 2.25
  exponential). This is the Live Score window, it is the least stable window, and it is not the
  one that counts.
- **Levels:** every method's score rises (for example `current_best` years 4–7 goes from 3.21 to
  4.39) because recent origins are easier relative to the 30-year reference (see below).

## Why

**Which metric drives the rank changes.** The top four methods (`current_best`, `hybrid_a`,
`hybrid_ridge_all_cdd`, `gev_stationary`) make **identical temperature forecasts** (the 10-year
mean). Their temperature skill is the same at every origin: years 4–7 7.52 % dense, 5.75 % early,
9.28 % late. So they can only be reordered by CDD, hottest day and precipitation:

| years 4–7 skill (%) | adr3 | dense | early ≤ 1995 | late > 1995 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| CDD `current_best` | 6.84 | 5.31 | 2.24 | 8.38 |
| CDD `hybrid_ridge_all_cdd` | **10.27** | **8.37** | **5.00** | **11.74** |
| CDD `hybrid_a` | 7.65 | 7.30 | 4.58 | 10.03 |
| CDD `arima_temp_cdd` | 7.16 | 5.50 | 2.69 | 8.31 |
| CDD `linear_trend` | −9.31 | −3.82 | −16.47 | 8.84 |
| hottest day `gev_stationary` / `hybrid_a` | +0.05 | −0.79 | −1.01 | −0.58 |
| temperature `arima_temp_cdd` | 4.77 | 4.61 | 4.37 | 4.85 |
| temperature `linear_trend` | −1.76 | −0.99 | −8.32 | 6.35 |
| temperature 10-year mean (top four) | 11.90 | 7.52 | 5.75 | 9.28 |

- The pick is decided by **CDD**. `hybrid_ridge_all_cdd` beats `current_best` on CDD by
  +2.8 points early and +3.4 late. The gain holds in both halves, so weighting cannot remove it.
- The one years 4–7 swap (`gev_stationary` vs `current_best`) is **hottest day**. The GEV median
  edges the 30-year mean on the ADR origins (+0.05) and loses on the dense set (−0.79).
- The years 1–3 swaps are **temperature** (`arima_temp_cdd`) and **CDD** (`hybrid_a`'s EWMA).
  Both gaps are strongly negatively correlated with the warming shift over the following years
  (ARIMA temperature-skill gap vs `shift_vs_10y`: −0.84; `hybrid_a` CDD gap: −0.62). They win
  when the next 1–3 years turn out cooler than the last decade, as after 1995 and 2007, and lose
  when the next years are warm. That is luck about short-term weather, which is why years 1–3
  rankings are unstable.

**Do warming-tracking methods gain at recent origins?** Only the true trend method does, and it
still finishes last.

- `linear_trend` gains **+4.5 raw-score points per decade of origin year** over `current_best`
  in years 4–7 (OLS across the 12 origins). Its temperature skill goes from −8.3 % (early) to
  +6.4 % (late), and its CDD skill from −16.5 % to +8.8 %. In the late half its CDD skill is
  slightly **above** `current_best`'s (8.84 against 8.38). It stays last because a 30-year line
  through precipitation and hottest day only extrapolates noise: −13.8 % and −9.9 % skill even
  at late origins.
- **Mechanism:** the 30-year city-mean temperature slope known at the cut-off changes sign.
  It is −0.28 °F/decade at 1980 and −0.13 at 1983, reflecting the mid-century plateau, then
  +0.27 to +0.59 from 1989 on. A line fitted at an early origin points the wrong way; after 1989
  it points the right way. **Check:** partly supported. The two origins with a negative
  pre-cutoff slope (1980, 1983) are `linear_trend`'s two worst for temperature in years 4–7
  (−35.9 % and −17.9 % skill). At later origins its skill still ranges from −12.2 % (2004) to
  +24.1 % (2011), and across all 12 origins the correlation of its temperature-skill gap with
  the pre-cutoff slope is only +0.37. Once the slope is positive, how
  much warming actually follows matters more (correlation with `shift_vs_10y` +0.45; for CDD
  +0.71).
- The **hybrids do not track warming**. `hybrid_ridge_all_cdd`'s gap to `current_best` has
  almost no trend with origin year (−0.04 points/decade in years 4–7). Its CDD-gap correlation
  with the pre-cutoff slope is −0.06, and with `shift_vs_10y` +0.25. **Mechanism:** the pooled
  ridge gives a better-calibrated *level* for CDD by learning, across 210 cities, how far
  each city's recent CDD should be trusted against its longer history. It does not extrapolate
  a trend. **Check:** supported. The gain is about the same size early (+2.8) and late (+3.4),
  and the ridge is first in years 4–7 in both halves. `hybrid_a`'s EWMA gain shrinks
  slightly over time (−0.16/decade), which is why it slips behind `current_best` over all years
  under exponential weights.
- `arima_temp_cdd` **loses ground** at recent origins (−0.61/decade against `current_best` in
  years 4–7). Its temperature skill is flat (4.4 % early, 4.9 % late) while the 10-year mean's
  rises from 5.8 % to 9.3 %. ARIMA's per-series models revert towards a longer-run level that
  lags the recent warming.
- **Why every score is higher at recent origins:** the reference is the 30-year mean. The
  faster the warming before the cut-off, the further that mean lags. Across origins, the
  years 4–7 raw-score gap `thirty_year_mean − current_best` correlates −0.65 with the
  pre-cutoff temperature slope and −0.81 with the pre-cutoff CDD slope. Recent origins make *every*
  recent-window method look good relative to the reference. Since the top methods share that
  advantage, it raises levels without reordering them.

**Why the ADR origins run high.** 1995 and 2011 are two of the three best origins for every
top method in years 4–7 (`current_best` 8.02 and 7.95, against a dense mean of 3.21). Both were
followed by strong warming against the 30-year mean (`shift_vs_30y` +0.91 °F and +1.67 °F,
CDD +71 and +211). Differences between methods carry over (`hybrid_ridge_all_cdd` − `current_best`
is +0.86 on adr3 and +0.77 dense), but absolute levels do not. Quote dense means when comparing
with anything other than another backtest on the same origins.

## Caveats

- **Overlap.** Consecutive dense origins share test years, so the 12 origins are not 12
  independent samples. The bootstrap and leave-one-out understate uncertainty. The 9 non-ADR
  origins give an independent check of the ADR ranking (tau 0.93 in years 4–7), but they are
  not independent of each other.
- **Same years, different horizon.** Every dense test year lies in 1981–2018, the same span as
  the ADR folds. Nothing here says how 2022–2025 will behave. Whether recent origins "better
  resemble" the Final Score is an assumption; the recency weights only test sensitivity to it.
- **Selection optimism carries over.** `hybrid_ridge_all_cdd` was the best of 15 round-1 variants
  picked on the ADR origins, and `hybrid_a` was assembled after seeing ADR results. The 9
  non-ADR origins are the cleanest out-of-selection test. On them, the ridge still leads in years
  4–7 (3.45 against `current_best` 2.71). Its pooled hyperparameters were not re-tuned.
- **No neural methods.** DeepAR and N-BEATS were left out for machine limits. Round 1's
  `deepar_hybrid` is the only temperature model that edged the 10-year mean; its ranking on the
  dense set is unknown.
- **Weights are ours, not the papers'.** Linear and 10-year-half-life exponential weights were
  fixed in the config before the first run; no other weightings were tried.
- **Papers.** P10's full text and DOI were not checked (the publisher page did not load). P19 is
  used from its abstract, P12 from the online textbook section. No numbers from them are used.
- **Process note.** During setup, one `uv run` call without the shared-environment exports
  briefly created a `.venv` inside this worktree. It was deleted within a minute, and the
  shared `.venv` was not touched.
