# Policy idea: Cooling-resilience planning from recent CDD normals

## What risk did we notice?

Cooling degree days translate temperature into the annual intensity of
air-conditioning demand. The model-selection evidence shows that recent
ten-year conditions are the most reliable simple signal for CDD and annual
temperature across the historical seven-year tests. Planning from a
thirty-year normal alone can therefore understate the cooling conditions a city
or utility must be ready to serve.

## What should happen, and who should do it?

City sustainability offices and electric utilities should publish an annual
**Cooling Resilience Trigger** based on each city's backtest-validated CDD and
annual-temperature forecast, compared with its 30-year baseline. When the
trigger is elevated, they should jointly:

1. reserve summer peak capacity and expand heat-emergency communications;
2. prioritize cool roofs, shade trees, and building-envelope retrofits in
   high-energy-burden areas using a separate local vulnerability assessment;
3. report avoided peak demand and program coverage before the next cooling
   season.

The station-level forecasts set the city-scale planning signal. Local agencies,
not this dataset, must supply neighborhood vulnerability and siting data before
targeting interventions.

## Why is it sustainable?

Avoiding cooling load through shade, reflective roofs, and efficiency reduces
both peak-grid stress and lifetime energy use. Reserving capacity earlier is
less costly and less emissions-intensive than emergency generation during heat
events. The proposal also avoids treating airport weather stations as a proxy
for neighborhood heat exposure, preserving the boundary between a city-scale
forecast and an equitable local implementation.
