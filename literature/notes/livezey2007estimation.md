*   **Type:** peer-reviewed article
*   **Relevance:** P01. Compares 30-yr WMO normals, optimal climate normals (OCN), linear trend fits and "hinge fit" normals for extrapolating U.S. climate a few years ahead.

## Contribution

*   **Research problem:** WMO 30-yr normals are unrepresentative of the current and near-future climate under rapid change.
*   **Method:** Compares OCN (variable-length recent averages), least squares linear trends since the mid-1970s, and a least squares hinge fit (flat 1940-1975, linear after 1975), on U.S. climate-division data.
*   **Data or materials:** U.S. climate-division seasonal temperature and precipitation records.
*   **Main result:** Simple empirical alternatives give reasonably accurate normals and often justify extrapolation several years ahead when recent trends are approximately linear; hinge fit is described as an even better alternative.

## Quotes

1.  "WMO-recommended 30-yr normals are no longer generally useful for the design, planning, and decisionmaking purposes for which they were intended. They not only have little relevance to the future climate, but are often unrepresentative of the current climate." (abstract, p. 1759)
    *   Supports: the authors judge fixed 30-yr normals poorly suited to representing current or future climate.
2.  "It is demonstrated that simple empirical alternatives already are available that not only produce reasonably accurate normals for the current climate but also often justify their extrapolation to several years into the future. This result is tied to the condition that recent trends in the climate are approximately linear or have a substantial linear component." (abstract)
    *   Supports: empirical alternatives can be extrapolated several years ahead, conditional on approximately linear recent trends.
3.  "The OCN works well except with very strong trends or longer extrapolations with more moderate trends. In these cases least squares linear trend fits to the period since the mid-1970s are viable alternatives. An even better alternative is the use of “hinge fit” normals, based on modeling the time dependence of large-scale climate change." (abstract)
    *   Supports: OCN degrades for strong trends or long extrapolations; trend fits since the mid-1970s and hinge fits are offered as alternatives.
4.  "This least squares hinge fit is a piecewise continuous function that is flat (i.e., constant) from 1940 through 1975 but slopes upward (or downward as dictated by the data) thereafter: Y(t) ⫽ a for 1940 ⱕ t ⱕ 1975 and Y(t) ⫽ a ⫹ b(t ⫺ 1975) for t ⱖ 1975." (section 4b, p. 1767 approx.; symbols as extracted by pdftotext, "⫽" is "=", "ⱕ"/"ⱖ" are "<="/">=", "⫹"/"⫺" are "+"/"-")
    *   Supports: the hinge-fit functional form with a 1975 change point.
5.  "The slope b is insensitive to small changes in this choice." (section 4b)
    *   Supports: the authors report the hinge slope is insensitive to small changes in the 1975 hinge point.
6.  "OCN is currently used at CPC for short-term climate prediction, ␶ ⱕ 1 yr, using empirically, not theoretically, estimated optimal averaging time intervals (for ␶ ⫽ 1 yr) fixed at 15 yr for monthly precipitation and 10 yr for monthly temperatures (Huang et al. 1996; Van den Dool 2006)." (p. 1765 approx.; "␶" is tau, the lead)
    *   Supports: CPC's operational OCN windows were 15 yr (precipitation) and 10 yr (temperature).
7.  "Fixed N is more convenient but is inadvisable unless Noptimal varies little across a user’s applications." (same passage)
    *   Supports: the authors advise against a fixed averaging length unless the optimal length varies little.
8.  "the authors recommend that (a) the WMO and national climate services address new policies for changing climate normals using the results here as a starting point and (b) NOAA initiate a program for improved estimates and forecasts of official U.S. normals, including operational implementation of a simple hybrid system that combines the advantages of both the OCN and the hinge fit." (abstract)
    *   Supports: the authors recommend a hybrid of OCN and hinge fit.

## Not Covered

*   Station-level (single airport) records; the study uses climate divisions.
*   Annual maxima of daily Tmax, cooling degree days, or MAE-based skill against a 30-yr mean in the target setting.
