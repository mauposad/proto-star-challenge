## Contribution

*   **Research problem:** Benchmark statistical models for decadal SST predictions against initialized GCMs.
*   **Method:** Per-grid-box linear regression of SST on equivalent global-mean CO2; residuals modeled by AR(1) or constructed analogue (CA).
*   **Data or materials:** HadISST gridded observations; 46 retrospective forecasts with start years 1960-2005; DePreSys for comparison.
*   **Main result:** Trend+AR1 correlation >0.7 in parts of the extra-tropical North Atlantic, Indian Ocean and western Pacific, mainly from the forced trend.

## Quotes

1.  "For both statistical models, the trend related to radiative forcing is modelled using a linear regression of SST time series at each grid box on the time series of equivalent global mean atmospheric CO2 concentration. The residual internal variability is then modelled by (1) a first-order autoregressive model (AR1) and (2) a constructed analogue model (CA)." (abstract)
    *   Supports: benchmark = CO2 regression trend plus AR1 or CA residual model.
2.  "From the verification of 46 retrospective forecasts with start years from 1960 to 2005, the correlation coefficient for anomaly forecasts using trend with AR1 is greater than 0.7 over parts of extra-tropical North Atlantic, the Indian Ocean and western Pacific. This is primarily related to the prediction of the forced trend." (abstract)
    *   Supports: much of the trend+AR1 skill comes from the forced trend.
3.  "DePreSys is found to outperform the statistical models over large parts of North Atlantic for lead times of 2–5 years and 6–9 years, however trend with AR1 is generally superior to DePreSys in the North Atlantic Current region" (abstract)
    *   Supports: the GCM beat the benchmarks over much of the North Atlantic, but trend+AR1 was better in one region.
