## Contribution

*   **Research problem:** Best way to estimate present/near-future climate means in a changing climate.
*   **Method:** Eight normals formulations compared in artificial- and real-data settings.
*   **Data or materials:** U.S. climate-division seasonal temperature and precipitation.
*   **Main result:** Most recent 15-yr (temperature) and 30-yr (precipitation) averages performed better in the recent past; hinge not yet justified.

## Quotes

1.  "Here eight formulations for climate normals, including the recently proposed “hinge” function, are compared in artificial- and real-data settings." (abstract)
    *   Supports: the study compares eight normals formulations including the hinge.
2.  "Although the hinge function is attractive conceptually for representing accelerating climate changes simply, its use is in general not yet justified for divisional U.S. seasonal temperature or precipitation." (abstract)
    *   Supports: the author did not find the hinge justified in general for divisional seasonal data.
3.  "Averages of the most recent 15 and 30 yr have performed better during the recent past for U.S. divisional seasonal temperature and precipitation, respectively; these averaging windows are longer than those currently employed for this purpose at the U.S. Climate Prediction Center." (abstract)
    *   Supports: 15-yr averages for temperature and 30-yr averages for precipitation performed better recently.
