## Contribution

*   **Research problem:** Why simple averages are often more robust than sophisticated combining.
*   **Method:** Analysis of explanations; simulations and real data; multi-level AFTER strategy.
*   **Data or materials:** Simulations and real data (details in paper).
*   **Main result:** Treating the simple average as a candidate in a multi-level AFTER scheme largely avoids estimation-error cost.

## Quotes

1.  "It is often reported in forecast combination literature that a simple average of candidate forecasts is more robust than sophisticated combining methods. This phenomenon is usually referred to as the “forecast combination puzzle”." (abstract)
    *   Supports: definition of the forecast combination puzzle.
2.  "We show that existing understanding of the puzzle should be complemented by the distinction of different forecast combination scenarios known as combining for adaptation and combining for improvement." (abstract)
    *   Supports: the authors' distinction between combining for adaptation and for improvement.
3.  "In particular, by treating the simple average as a candidate forecast, the proposed strategy is shown to avoid the heavy cost of estimation error and, to a large extent, solve the forecast combination puzzle." (abstract)
    *   Supports: including the simple average as a candidate mitigates estimation error.
