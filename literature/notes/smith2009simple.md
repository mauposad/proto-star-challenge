## Contribution

*   **Research problem:** Why simple combinations beat sophisticated weighted combinations.
*   **Method:** Monte Carlo study, reappraisal of Stock and Watson (2003), large-sample variance approximation.
*   **Data or materials:** Simulations; Stock and Watson empirical application.
*   **Main result:** Finite-sample error in estimating weights explains the puzzle.

## Quotes

1.  "This article presents a formal explanation of the forecast combination puzzle, that simple combinations of point forecasts are repeatedly found to outperform sophisticated weighted combinations in empirical applications. The explanation lies in the effect of finite‐sample error in estimating the combining weights." (abstract)
    *   Supports: the puzzle and its weight-estimation-error explanation.
2.  "The Monte Carlo evidence, together with a large‐sample approximation to the variance of the combining weight, also supports the popular recommendation to ignore forecast error covariances in estimating the weight." (abstract)
    *   Supports: recommendation to ignore error covariances when estimating weights.
