*   **Type:** report (assessment chapter)
*   **Relevance:** Observed U.S. annual precipitation trends are small nationally and regionally mixed, with large interannual variability.

## Contribution

*   **Research problem:** Assess observed and projected U.S. precipitation change.
*   **Method:** Assessment of station records and CMIP5 projections.
*   **Data or materials:** Long-term station observations; proxies; CMIP5.
*   **Main result:** ~4% national increase in annual precipitation since 1901, mostly fall; decreases in West/Southwest/Southeast, increases in Plains/Midwest/Northeast.

## Quotes

1.  "Annual precipitation has decreased in much of the West, Southwest, and Southeast and increased in most of the Northern and Southern Plains, Midwest, and Northeast. A national average increase of 4% in annual precipitation since 1901 is mostly a result of large increases in the fall season. (Medium confidence)" (Key Finding 1)
    *   Supports: regionally mixed signs of annual precipitation trends and a small national increase.
2.  "Annual precipitation averaged across the United States has increased approximately 4% over the 1901–2015 period, slightly less than the 5% increase reported in the Third National Climate Assessment (NCA3) over the 1901–2012 period." (section 7.1.1)
    *   Supports: size of the national trend and its sensitivity to end year.
3.  "Interannual variability is substantial, as evidenced by large multiyear meteorological and agricultural droughts in the 1930s and 1950s." (section 7.1.1)
    *   Supports: large interannual variability in U.S. precipitation.

## Not Covered
