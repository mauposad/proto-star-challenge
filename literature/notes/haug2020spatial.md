# haug2020spatial

*   **Citation:** Haug, O., Thorarinsdottir, T. L., Sørbye, S. H., & Franzke, C. L. E. (2020). Spatial trend analysis of gridded temperature data at varying spatial scales. Advances in Statistical Climatology, Meteorology and Oceanography 6, 1-12.
*   **Identifier:** DOI 10.5194/ascmo-6-1-2020; OpenAlex W2913020974 — https://doi.org/10.5194/ascmo-6-1-2020
*   **Retrieved:** 2026-11-09
*   **Access:** full text (`sources/haug2020spatial.pdf`, `sources/haug2020spatial.txt`)
*   **Type:** peer-reviewed article
*   **Relevance:** P22. Hierarchical Bayesian space-time model with trend coefficients as a latent Gaussian random field (spatial partial pooling of trends).

## Contribution

*   **Research problem:** Grid-cell-independent trend tests ignore spatial correlation.
*   **Method:** Space-time hierarchical Bayesian model; trend coefficient as latent Gaussian random field; simultaneous credible regions.
*   **Data or materials:** 65 years of gridded summer temperature over Europe.
*   **Main result:** Spatial smoothing generally enlarges regions where no-trend is rejected, but not in all subregions.

## Quotes

1.  "Classical assessments of trends in gridded temperature data perform independent evaluations across the grid, thus, ignoring spatial correlations in the trend estimates." (abstract)
    *   Supports: motivation against independent per-location trend estimates.
2.  "we build a space–time hierarchical Bayesian model for temperature anomalies where the trend coefficient is modelled by a latent Gaussian random field." (abstract)
    *   Supports: trends are spatially pooled via a latent random field.
3.  "We find that while spatial smoothing generally results in larger regions where the null hypothesis of no trend is rejected, this is not the case for all subregions." (abstract)
    *   Supports: pooling changes significance regions, not uniformly.
